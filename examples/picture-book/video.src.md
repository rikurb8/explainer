# The Bench at Night

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch01.svg}}
:::

:::narration
Mara writes small things. A gate. A lamp. A bird that sits on the gate and will not move for anything.

Every night the bench fills with pages. The writing she knows. The making takes all night: a cover, eleven pictures, a voice, one long reel.

She never finishes before morning. So she stops, and she leaves a note.
:::

# The Note on the Bench

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch02.svg}}
:::

:::narration
The note sits on top of the pile, and it is small. The Book of Small Things. Cover: a bird, a gate, a lamp. For Ada, who is five.

She does not write it for herself. She knows what she meant. A helper can only make what someone wrote down.
:::

# Something Comes Up the Leg

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch03.svg}}
:::

:::narration
At midnight the bench leg gets visitors. Tick comes first, blue and quick, with the lantern held high. Tock follows, smaller and slower.

They read the note before they touch a single page. Every night, in the same order. A bird. A gate. A lamp. For Ada, who is five.
:::

# What They Make by Morning

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch04.svg}}
:::

:::narration
By morning there are two things on the bench. A book: a cover, a contents, eleven chapters, a picture beside the words.

And a reel: the same eleven pages, held on a screen one at a time, while a voice reads them and leaves a silence at the end of each.

One pile of pages. Two things to hold. Neither arrives in one flash. Each is a row of small steps, done in order.
:::

# Three Dials

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch05.svg}}
:::

:::narration
The machine has three dials. How many pages. How fast the voice reads. Whose voice it is.

That is everything there is to set. Turn them. Nothing breaks, and nothing is spent. A run that comes out wrong is only a run you can do again.
:::

# Too Fast

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch06.svg}}
:::

:::narration
One night Tock turned the pace dial too far. One point nine. The words ran together like a river after rain.

The pages flew past: a gate, a bird, a lamp, gone. Ada laughed, and then stopped laughing, because she could not follow it.
:::

# Too Slow

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch07.svg}}
:::

:::narration
The next night Tock turned the same dial the other way. Zero point three.

One page stayed so long that a spider built a web across the corner of it. The plant grew a leaf. Ada came back after breakfast, and the page was still there.
:::

# Just Right

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch08.svg}}
:::

:::narration
One point zero. The voice reads at the speed of a person reading to a child, and then it stops, for one beat, at the end of the page.

That beat is the whole thing. It is the space where a child looks at the picture. It is where the page is allowed to land.
:::

# The Rules the Elves Keep

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch09.svg}}
:::

:::narration
One idea a page. A page that says two things says neither of them.

The picture shows what the words cannot. A gate is a word. A bird that will not move is a picture.

The words say it out loud. And nothing is made that was not written down.
:::

# Mara Stays Awake

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch10.svg}}
:::

:::narration
One night Mara did not go to bed. She watched them read the note, cut the pile into pages, and hand each page to the voice.

Then the long part: frame after frame, held still, stitched into a single reel. It took all night. It was not magic. It was an order.
:::

# The Last Small Note

:::figure wide
{{figures/ch11.svg}}
:::

:::narration hold=1
The next night Mara stayed at the bench. She wrote the note, turned the dials, pressed run, and went to sleep while it worked.

In the morning there was a folded note she had not written. You know the order now. She kept it with the good pencils.
:::
