# One plant, months of payoff

:::narration
A chili plant starts as a seed the size of a pepper flake. Give it warmth, light, and a few patient months, and it pays you back in pods far hotter than anything at the supermarket.

This video follows one season. Pick a variety, start the seeds, keep the plant happy, and harvest at the right moment.
:::

# Pick your heat

:::flow animate | Chili heat is measured in Scoville units.
row
box jalapeno "Jalapeño" "Mild, ~5,000 SHU" muted
box cayenne "Cayenne" "Medium, ~50,000 SHU" accent
box habanero "Habanero" "Hot, ~250,000 SHU" blue
arrow jalapeno -> cayenne
arrow cayenne -> habanero
:::

:::narration
Chili heat is measured in Scoville units. A jalapeño sits around five thousand. A habanero can pass two hundred and fifty thousand, fifty times hotter.

First-time growers should start mild. Jalapeños and cayennes fruit generously, forgive uneven watering, and still teach you everything a hotter plant would.
:::

# Start early, stay warm

```
Sow indoors:     8 weeks before the last frost
Germination:     25-30 °C soil, 7-21 days
Transplant out:  nights reliably above 15 °C
```

:::narration
Chilies are slow to start. Sow indoors about eight weeks before your last frost date, and keep the soil warm, around twenty-five to thirty degrees Celsius.

A heat mat makes a real difference here. Germination can take three weeks, so label your trays and resist the urge to dig around checking on them.
:::

# The seedling weeks

:::sequence animate | Seedlings need light and one upgrade each.
actor tray "Seed tray" muted
actor light "Grow light" accent
actor grower "You" blue
tray -> grower: keep soil moist, never soggy
light -> tray: 14 hours a day, close above
grower -> tray: pot up at the second leaf pair
:::

:::narration
Once seedlings emerge, light becomes the priority. Keep a grow light a few centimeters above them for about fourteen hours a day, or they stretch tall and floppy.

When each seedling has two pairs of true leaves, move it to its own pot. Bury it a little deeper than before and it will grow a stronger stem.
:::

# Sun, water, food

:::flow animate | A weekly rhythm once the plant is outside.
row
box sun "Sun" "6-8 hours, full sun" blue
box water "Water" "When the topsoil dries" accent
box feed "Feed" "Half-strength, low nitrogen" muted
arrow sun -> water
arrow water -> feed
:::

:::narration
Transplant out only once nights stay above fifteen degrees. Then give the plant the sunniest spot you have, at least six hours a day.

Water when the top centimeter of soil dries out, and feed little and often. Too much nitrogen grows a beautiful leafy bush with almost no pods.
:::

# Flowers become pods

:::sequence animate | From white flower to red pod.
actor plant "Plant" muted
actor flower "White flowers" accent
actor pod "Pods" blue
flower -> plant: open in the morning
plant -> flower: gentle shake pollinates them
flower --> pod: fruit sets after a few days
pod --> plant: turns from green to its final color
:::

:::narration
Flowers appear in clusters and usually pollinate themselves. If you grow indoors, a gentle shake of the plant each morning does the job of the breeze and the bees.

Some flowers drop when the plant is stressed. That is normal, and it stops once watering is steady. A set pod swells fast, then ripens over weeks.
:::

# Harvest, then do it again

```
Pick:    when the pod reaches its final color
How:     cut the stem, do not pull
Bonus:   overwinter the plant indoors for
         an earlier, bigger crop next year
```

:::narration
Green pods are edible, but full flavor and heat arrive with the final color. Cut the stem with scissors rather than pulling, so the branch keeps flowering.

And here is the best trick: chilies are perennial. Bring the plant indoors before frost, water it sparingly over winter, and it returns in spring, bigger than any seedling.
:::
